Muscle Mosaic

A cross-section of ~950 fibers & 50 motor units — recruitment, rate coding, fatigue and recovery, live.

About the model

Henneman's size principle. Motor units are recruited smallest-first as force demand rises: slow Type I units first, then fast fatigue-resistant Type IIa, then fast fatigable Type IIx. Derecruitment happens in reverse order.

Rate coding. Once recruited, a unit's firing rate rises (~6→50 Hz) with drive, ramping its force from ~20% to 100% of its capacity. Recruitment is complete by ~88% MVC; rate coding does the rest.

Fatigue. Active fibers accumulate fatigue that scales their force down. Type IIx fatigues ~20× faster than Type I; IIa is in between.

Recovery. At rest, fatigue decays exponentially — but the fast-fatiguing IIx fibers recover fastest (τ ≈ 30 s, think PCr/pH restoration), while slow fibers that have fatigued recover slowest (τ ≈ 2.5 min). The fibers that did the work need the rest.

Maintain-force mode models what happens when you hold a position: the controller raises neural drive to compensate for fatiguing fibers, so you watch recruitment climb until the muscle can no longer hit the target — contractile failure. Fixed-drive mode holds drive constant and lets force sag.

Simplifications: one fatigue scalar per fiber, lumped force–frequency curve, uniform MU territories, no potentiation/LEP, fiber counts scaled down ~100×. Hover any fiber to inspect its motor unit.
